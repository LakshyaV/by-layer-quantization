Items that could not be fetched are marked **UNVERIFIED**. Where a claim is my synthesis rather than a source's, I say so.

**TL;DR up front, because you asked for bluntness:** the idea as originally framed — per-layer damage scan → fragility map → non-uniform bits beat uniform — is substantially published and, worse for us, substantially *built as free community tooling in the exact llama.cpp ecosystem we planned to use*. The honest inventory is below. Section 8 says what survives; something real does, but it is narrower and sharper than the original pitch, and it has a clock on it.

Terms used throughout: **PTQ** = post-training quantization (compressing a trained model without retraining). **RTN** = round-to-nearest (the simplest PTQ: scale, round, done — your sketch). **k-quants** = llama.cpp's block-quantization formats (Q2_K…Q6_K). **GGUF** = llama.cpp's model file format. **imatrix** = llama.cpp's "importance matrix," activation statistics used to weight the rounding *within* a tensor. **KL divergence** = a measure of how much one probability distribution differs from another; here, how far the damaged model's next-token distribution drifts from the fp16 model's.

## 1. The idea's ancestry: Hessian-era sensitivity → mixed precision (2019–2022)

"Measure per-layer sensitivity, allocate bits non-uniformly" is a five-year-old paradigm. The classic line measures sensitivity with a *proxy* (curvature of the loss surface via the Hessian) rather than by ablation:

- **HAWQ** (2019) — https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03696 — picks each layer's relative precision from its Hessian spectrum (top eigenvalue). CNNs; manual final bit selection.
- **HAWQ-V2** (NeurIPS 2020) — https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03852 — better sensitivity = Hessian *trace*; automatic allocation via a Pareto-frontier method.
- **HAWQ-V3** (ICML 2021) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.10680 — bit allocation as an **integer linear program** under memory/latency constraints. This is structurally the same optimization our validation step will run (utility per tensor, budget in bytes).
- **Q-BERT** (AAAI 2020) — https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05840 — first Hessian-aware mixed precision on a transformer (BERT), with group-wise quantization.
- **ZeroQuant** (2022) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01861 — PTQ machinery for GPT-style models; module-type precision splits (INT4 FC / INT8 attention), not a measured map.

**Takeaway:** the *skeleton* (sensitivity score → budgeted allocation) is textbook. Novelty cannot live there. What was still open when these were written: sensitivity by *direct ablation* rather than proxy, on modern decoder LLMs, with an output-distribution metric.

## 2. LLM-era salience: protecting important weights is standard practice

- **LLM.int8()** (NeurIPS 2022) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07339 — emergent outlier *feature dimensions* dominate predictive performance; keep them fp16, quantize the rest. Damage is concentrated and structured — the founding observation behind any fragility map.
- **AWQ** (MLSys 2024, best paper) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00978 — protecting ~1% of salient channels (salience from **activation** statistics on calibration data) hugely reduces error; protection via scaling, explicitly avoiding mixed precision. Note: salience is implicitly conditioned on the calibration set — the seed of task-conditioning, never developed there.
- **SqueezeLLM** (2023) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07629 — sensitivity-based *non-uniform codebooks* within tensors + sparse outlier storage.
- **OWQ** (AAAI 2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02272 — mixed precision at *column* granularity (weak columns kept high-precision).
- **SpQR** (2023) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03078 — isolate outlier weights in higher precision, 3–4 bit for the rest, near-lossless.
- **GPTQ** (2022) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.17323 — smarter within-tensor rounding via approximate second-order information. Orthogonal to allocation; relevant as the reason our sweep should use deployment-grade rounding (see EXPERIMENT.md §2), not naive RTN.
- **Massive Activations** (COLM 2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17762 — 2–4 activations per sequence are up to ~100,000× larger than the rest; they appear abruptly at an early layer (layer 2 in LLaMA-2-7B), persist, and fade in the last layers; zeroing them collapses the model. Predicts a *layer-shaped* fragility profile.
- **The Super Weight in LLMs** (Apple, 2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07191 — a *handful of scalar weights* (often 1–3 per model, **always in `mlp.down_proj` in an early layer**) whose removal destroys the model (perplexity ×1000). The closest philosophical kin to fragility mapping: an ablate-one-thing-at-a-time sensitivity study, but at scalar granularity, task-agnostic, and locating only catastrophic points. Note the striking rhyme: Q4_K_M's folklore rule *also* gives early `ffn_down` layers extra bits (see §6). Nobody has connected these two facts in print — see §8.
- **Attention sinks** (ICLR 2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453 — initial tokens absorb disproportionate attention regardless of content; background on position-level load-bearing structure.

**Takeaway:** "some parts matter much more than others, and you can find them with activation/ablation evidence" is established at every granularity *except* the one llama.cpp actually allocates at — the tensor.

## 3. Layer-importance maps from the pruning literature (the "known picture" risk)

This is the literature that makes a bare fragility heatmap unpublishable:

- **The Unreasonable Ineffectiveness of the Deeper Layers** (2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17887 — up to ~half of layers (the deeper ones) can be *removed* with minor QA damage after light healing. The coarse shape "first/last layers matter, deep middle is soft" is already public.
- **ShortGPT** (2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03853 — per-layer Block Influence score; layer removal beats prior structured pruning.
- **LaCo** (EMNLP 2024 Findings) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11187 — 25–30% of layers merged away, >80% performance kept.
- **What Matters in Transformers? Not All Attention is Needed** (2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15786 — attention layers are far more redundant than MLPs (half of Llama-2-70B's attention layers pruned ⇒ 2.4% drop). Module-type asymmetry is known.
- **Structural Sensitivity in Compressed Transformers** (Mar 2026) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20991 — per-component compression sensitivity across 6 models: ~600× spread, **early MLP up-projections catastrophic, value projections nearly free**, and importance scores *don't transfer across architectures*. Pruning-oriented, no bit allocation — but this is a tensor-granular sensitivity result that partially overlaps our map.
- **TELL-TALE** (ACL 2026 Findings) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22767 — **task-aware** layer *elimination*: which layers to drop depends on the task; some layers are actively harmful for specific tasks. Task-conditioned layer importance exists in print — on the pruning side.

**Takeaway:** if our map's story is "layers differ in importance, roughly by depth and module type," that is a redraw of published results. The map must carry information these papers don't have: tensor-level structure, *quantization-specific* fragility (a layer can be un-prunable yet quant-robust, or vice versa), task-conditioning, and a validated allocation payoff.

## 4. Metric precedent: KL over accuracy and perplexity

Good news: our metric choice is well-supported. Bad news: it's supported because others published it first.

- **Accuracy is Not All You Need** (2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09141 — compressed models matching baseline accuracy still behave differently (correct↔incorrect "flips"); proposes **KL divergence and flip rate** as the right divergence metrics. (Affiliation not shown on the arXiv page; I won't attribute it.)
- **A KL Lens on Quantization** (Apr 2026) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13440 — **this is our core loop, published**: quantize one layer at a time, compute KL of logits vs the full-precision model, rank, allocate mixed precision; includes a formal argument that KL beats MSE and SQNR as a sensitivity signal. Mitigation: it targets **SSM/hybrid models** (Mamba/Mamba2/Hymba/Zamba, ≤1.4B), INT4/INT8/FP16 thresholding, and only uniform baselines — no standard transformer LLMs, no k-quants, no strong mixed baseline, no tasks.
- **The Illusion of Equivalency** (Jul 2026) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08734 — accuracy-preserving quants still shift decisions; claims **Q/K projections are more quantization-sensitive than V/O** — a per-tensor-type claim that *partially contradicts* the k-quant folklore (which bumps V). Our map will confirm or refute this directly; either way, a citable dialogue.
- **Establishing a Scale for KL Divergence in LLMs** (May 2025) — arXiv 2505.15353 — **existence verified, details UNVERIFIED** (not fetched); reportedly calibrates what KL magnitudes mean. Useful for interpreting our numbers if it holds up.
- Reference KL magnitudes from llama.cpp's own tooling (perplexity README, verified): on Llama-3-8B/Wikitext-2, mean KLD vs fp16 is **0.031 for Q4_K_M**, 0.0055 for Q6_K, 0.0014 for Q8_0, 0.445 for Q2_K; the fp16-vs-bf16 noise floor is **0.000025**. These anchor our effect sizes and our "below this is noise" threshold.

## 5. Task-conditioned sensitivity: crowded, but unfinished

This was your candidate "adjacent open question." It is **partially taken** — two close papers appeared in the last ten months — but the tensor-granularity version is open, and the two papers are in tension with each other, which is an opportunity.

- **TAQ — "You Had One Job: Per-Task Quantization Using LLMs' Hidden Representations"** (Nov 2025; abstract states ICML 2026 acceptance) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06516 — the direct hit. Training-free per-task mixed precision: from ~512 unlabeled task prompts, score per-**layer** sensitivity (one variant injects quantization-scale *noise* into one layer at a time and measures output KL — a proxy, not real quantization), then give the top-25% layers 8-bit and the rest 4-bit. Tasks: TriviaQA (retrieval), CodeMMLU (code), MMLU-Pro (reasoning). Beats GPTQ/AWQ/SliM-LLM on Qwen2.5-3B/7B, Gemma-2-9B. **Crucial self-report: 87.5% of layer bit-assignments are identical across tasks (Spearman ρ=0.757)** — at layer granularity, the task-conditioned signal is *mostly shared*, concentrated at boundary layers. No GGUF, no equal-file-size baseline, no per-tensor granularity.
- **TASA — "Beyond Activation Alignment"** (Jul 2026) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00908 — task-aware calibration + bit allocation. Two findings that matter to us: (1) the **"Perplexity Illusion"** — layers ranked important by perplexity correlate ~zero (Kendall τ≈0) with layers important for *reasoning*; (2) the **alignment-diversity tradeoff** — calibrating *only* on target-task data can hurt; mixing in general data stabilizes sensitivity estimates. Task-aware 3.5-bit ≥ uniform 4-bit (+20 GSM8K points over W3 on Llama-3-8B). Layer granularity, academic formats, no GGUF.
- **Tension worth exploiting (my synthesis):** TAQ says task maps are ~87% shared; TASA says perplexity-vs-reasoning importance is uncorrelated. Both are layer-level, few tasks, different probes. A careful per-**tensor** study with matched methodology can adjudicate — and *either* outcome is a finding.
- **Why Do Some Inputs Break Low-Bit Quantization?** (EMNLP 2025) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12044 — which *examples* degrade is highly systematic across methods (r=0.82); hard examples depend on precise late-layer residual activations, MLP gate outputs critical. Input-conditioned (not task-conditioned) damage structure, with layer-level attribution.
- **Capability-uneven degradation (motivation, no maps):** multilingual — https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03211 (EMNLP 2024 Findings: non-Latin scripts hit worst; automatic metrics understate damage, 1.7% automatic vs 16% human-evaluated in Japanese); reasoning models — https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04823 (COLM 2025: W8A8/W4A16 lossless, below that math/science degrade, on R1-distills/QwQ/Qwen3-8B); math CoT — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11574 (localizes damage to *reasoning-step space*, not parameter space); extreme low-bit failure modes — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02011 (2-bit Qwen3: repetition loops; targeted "loop rescue" recovers MATH-500 17%→74%).
- **Calibration-data dependence:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09755 (ACL 2024: first broad study; calibration choice causes substantial downstream variation — the lever is real) and https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17711 (ICLR 2025: at high sparsity, data *similar to pretraining* works best — pushing *against* task-specialization). Note neither covers llama.cpp's imatrix specifically; imatrix-calibration lore is GitHub-discussion folklore (see §6).
- **Interp motivation** that tasks route through distinct components: induction heads — https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11895; function vectors (middle-layer attention heads carrying task identity) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15213.

## 6. llama.cpp archaeology: what k-quants are, and how Q4_K_M was actually chosen

Primary sources: PR #1684 (k-quants, June 2023), current `src/llama-quant.cpp`, `tools/quantize/quantize.cpp`, imatrix PR #4861 and README, perplexity README — all fetched and quoted 2026-08-26.

**Block structure** (why EXPERIMENT.md matches it): k-quants use 256-element **superblocks**. Q4_K = 8 sub-blocks of 32 weights, asymmetric (scale+min per sub-block), sub-block scales/mins themselves quantized to **6 bits** → 4.5 bits/weight effective. Q3_K = 16×16 sub-blocks, symmetric, 6-bit scales → 3.4375 bpw. Q6_K = 16×16, 8-bit scales → 6.5625 bpw. Q2_K → 2.625 bpw. Q8_0 → 8.5 bpw.

**How the Q4_K_M mix chooses per-tensor types** (current `llama-quant.cpp`, verbatim rule):

```cpp
auto use_more_bits = [](int i_layer, int n_layers) -> bool {
    return i_layer < n_layers/8 || i_layer >= 7*n_layers/8 || (i_layer - n_layers/8)%3 == 2;
};
```

For Q4_K_M: `attn_v` and `ffn_down` → Q6_K on those layers (first eighth, last eighth, every third in between); `output.weight` (and tied embeddings) → Q6_K; everything else Q4_K. Plus 2023-era special cases hardcoded to specific models: LLaMA-2-70B GQA ("we can get a nice boost … with nearly negligible increase in model size"), Mixtral `n_expert == 8` → Q8_0 with the comment **`// TODO: explore better strategies`**. I reproduced this live on Llama-3.2-1B during the spike: bumped layers were exactly {0,1,4,7,10,13,14,15}.

**Measured or folklore — the verdict, with nuance.** The PR contains **whole-model wikitext perplexity tables for LLaMA-1 7B/13B/30B/65B only**. There is **no per-tensor ablation table anywhere**, and no explanation for why specific tensors get more bits beyond one aside (without the bumps, Q2_K perplexity "becomes very high (around 9.0 for the 7B model)"). The heuristics cite no data, and are locked in place by a *snapshot test* of their own output (`tests/test-quant-type-selection.cpp`), not by any quality benchmark. The same June-2023 rules are applied unchanged to Qwen3 and Llama 3.2 today. And ikawrakow himself treats the stock mixes as stale: in his fork (ik_llama.cpp) he hand-builds new per-model recipes for Qwen3 and added `--custom-q` for exactly this purpose. **So the defensible claim is not "arbitrary folklore" but: *no published per-tensor measurement justifies Q4_K_M's allocation on any model, and none exists at all for post-2023 architectures.*** That is precisely the beatable surface — but see §7 before celebrating: several groups are already mining it.

**imatrix** (PR #4861, Jan 2024): weights the within-block least-squares fit by mean squared activations ⟨a²⟩. **It does not change per-tensor type allocation for k-quant ftypes** (verified in source: `has_imatrix` touches only three IQ/legacy branches). So allocation and rounding are cleanly separable — our experiments hold rounding (imatrix) fixed and vary only allocation. imatrix calibration-data choice is studied only in GitHub discussions (#5263: informal KLD comparisons; "no consistent improvement from using more Wikitext tokens") — no rigorous published study for imatrix specifically.

**Tooling we get for free** (all verified in the installed brew build 0.3.0 during today's spike):
- `llama-quantize --tensor-type "regex=type"` (PR #12511, EAddario, merged 2025-04-13; regex substring match, first match wins, logs `applying manual override`), `--tensor-type-file`, `--pure` (kill the stock mix), `--dry-run` (exact final size without quantizing — our byte-budget oracle), `--output-tensor-type`, `--token-embedding-type`.
- `llama-perplexity --kl-divergence-base` (save fp16 logits) + `--kl-divergence` (score a quant against them; reports mean KLD, Δp percentiles, same-top-p) + `--hellaswag`, `--multiple-choice`. Caveat: the logits file is ~2 bytes × vocab per token → **~300 KB/token for Qwen3's 151k vocab; a 30k-token probe ≈ 9 GB** — budget disk, keep probes tight, delete after.
- Spike result (today, Llama-3.2-1B): stock Q4_K_M = 763 MiB = **5.18 effective bpw** — nominal "4-bit" is fiction on small tied-embedding models; all comparisons must be in bytes, as you said.

## 8. What's actually novel here

**Blunt version: of the four claims in your original pitch, three are taken.**

1. ~~"Quantize one layer at a time, measure damage, build a map"~~ — published: KL Lens (2604.13440) does exactly this loop with exactly our metric, on SSM hybrids; TAQ does a noise-proxy version per layer on Qwen2.5; ScaleBITS does the mirror direction; Thireus ships it as a tool with per-tensor KLD.
2. ~~"Uniform quantization assumes all layers are equal — challenge that"~~ — nobody believes uniform is optimal; llama.cpp *shipped* non-uniform allocation in June 2023, and the pruning literature published the coarse layer-importance picture in 2024.
3. ~~"Use the map to beat uniform quantization at equal size"~~ — beating *uniform* is beating a strawman (you already caught this); beating *stock mixes* is done monthly by hobbyists (eaddario, ubergarm, Thireus users) and once at research grade (EvoPress-GGUF vs Unsloth).
4. **"Task-conditioned fragility"** — your adjacent-question guess — is *half* taken: TAQ and TASA own "task-aware per-layer allocation beats task-agnostic" at layer granularity in academic formats. Not taken at tensor granularity, not in a deployable format, and their own results disagree about how big the task effect is.

**What survives, ranked by value-per-week:**

- **(a) The tensor-granularity task question — the recommended headline.** TAQ's own numbers say 87.5% of *layer* assignments are task-invariant; TASA says perplexity-important ≠ reasoning-important layers. Below the layer — across `attn_q/k/v/o`, `ffn_gate/up/down` — nobody has measured whether task-conditioned fragility structure exists. Super-weights (always early `ffn_down`), massive-activation sites, and function-vector middle layers all *predict* tensor-level, unevenly-task-loaded structure. The question "**how much fragility structure lives below the layer level, does it move with task, and does exploiting it pay at equal file size in a real format?**" is open, decision-relevant, and answerable in a week on your hardware. Either answer publishes: strong tensor-level task structure contradicts TAQ's invariance; near-invariance is a clean negative that settles the TAQ/TASA tension and *still* leaves you deliverable (b).
- **(b) The measured-map vs folklore validation.** No published per-tensor measurement justifies Q4_K_M's allocation on any model, none exists for post-2023 architectures, and the community tools attacking it all use proxies (imatrix stats, weighted MSE, SNR) — with documented misfires (eaddario's Qwen3-8B). A *measured full-model-KL* map on Qwen3-1.7B / Llama-3.2-3B / Qwen3-8B, realized as real GGUFs at byte-equal budgets against stock Q4_K_M **and against a folklore+ arm and a proxy arm**, is the rigorous version of what the ecosystem is doing by feel. This is the engineering result, and it de-risks the startup wedge. It is *not* a paper on its own — as a paper it would be "we did what Thireus does, carefully, on smaller models" — but as the validation clause of (a) it's load-bearing.
- **(c) A methodology finding, nearly free:** isolation-direction (quantize one, rest clean) vs marginal-direction (all quantized, bump one) sensitivity — ScaleBITS asserts the marginal direction is the right one; nobody has measured how much they disagree on the same models, or whether one-at-a-time scores predict joint damage (additivity). Our Sweep A/Sweep B design answers this as a by-product, and it's the kind of careful methods result reviewers reward.
- **(d) A mechanistic garnish, cheap:** overlay the map on known structures — do the fragile tensors coincide with super-weight layers and massive-activation creation sites? Did 2023 folklore accidentally bump the super-weight layers (early `ffn_down`)? One figure, high memorability.
- **(e) The map as a public artifact** (interactive per-tensor fragility maps for popular consumer models + the recipes) — not novelty, but distribution: it's the demo, the startup surface, and the thing r/LocalLLaMA can use the day it ships.

**The honest discouraging sentence you asked for:** if the week's result is "the map shows first/last layers and `ffn_down` matter, task maps are ~95% correlated, and our mix beats Q4_K_M by an amount within noise" — then this was a replication exercise, the paper dies, and the startup thesis (workload-specific compression) loses its scientific leg. That outcome is plausible; TAQ's 87.5% number is pointing at it. The plan (PLAN.md) is built so you'd see most of that by **end of Day 2** rather than month three.

**Startup read (one paragraph, since you chose both deliverables — superseded in part by §9):** "better GGUFs" as a product is commoditized by free hobbyist tooling and Unsloth's marketing machine — do not build that company. The surviving wedge is **workload-conditioned compression with certified equal-size wins** ("send us your traffic distribution, get back a model provably better *for your workload* at the same bytes") — which only exists as a business if the task-conditioned effect at tensor granularity is real and material. That is exactly what the week measures. Multiverse's $215M shows compression-as-product has venture appetite; TAQ/TASA show academia is circling; nobody yet sells the workload-specific version. The week is the due diligence.

## 9. Addendum (same day): second-round scoop-checks and the revised recommendation

After §8, two candidate "what else" directions were scoop-checked with the same verified-primary-source discipline (fetched 2026-08-26).

**Interaction/non-additivity of quantization damage: the layer-level version was answered in 2025–2026.**
- **Hill, "Saturation Makes Quantization Error Additive"** (Jul 2026, single-author, apparently unreviewed) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12266 — measures damage over **exhaustive 2^L subsets** of 8–10 contiguous mid-depth layers (RTN/GPTQ/FP4 ladders; Qwen3 0.6B–235B, Llama-3.2-3B, GLM up to 753B), decomposes by interaction order (functional ANOVA): per-layer effects explain ~85–93% of variance, and the residual fits a **uniform saturation/coverage model** (each quantized layer consumes a fixed fraction of remaining damage headroom — systematic subadditivity, no pair-specific structure); builds a knapsack allocator beating NVIDIA ModelOpt by 17–21% KL at matched memory.
- **CoopQ/IMPQ** (ACL Findings 2026) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15455 — Shapley-estimated pairwise inter-layer interaction terms + binary quadratic program for 2/4-bit per-layer allocation; 20–80% ppl reduction below 4 bits.
- **CLADO** (2023) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05657 — pairwise cross-layer terms + integer quadratic program, CNNs/ViTs only. **EvoPress** (ICML 2025) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14649 — published the observation that summed per-layer error mispredicts joint damage. **QEP** (NeurIPS 2025) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09629 — error accumulation across depth as a calibration fix.
- **Still open:** interactions at **tensor** granularity; **pair typology** (same-layer attn↔FFN vs adjacent vs distant — Hill's lattices are contiguous layer blocks); the **sign** of interactions (cancellation vs compounding through the residual stream — Hill's model is sign-uniform subadditive *by construction*); GGUF k-quant formats. Framing: a small structured pair sweep is a *refine-or-refute-Hill secondary claim* (C5 extension), not a headline. Read Hill skeptically — unreviewed.

**Fragility maps across training stages: verified open — the center is unclaimed. This is the new headline (#1).**
- **Training Dynamics Impact PTQ Robustness** (ICLR 2026) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06213 — tracks quantization degradation across SmolLM3's actual training stages (mid-training, long-context, SFT, APO): stage axis exists, but **whole-model only, explicitly no localization**.
- **QuantLRM** (2026) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02581 — uses ΔW = |W_finetuned − W_base| as a channel-level protection heuristic on R1-distills and RL-tuned models (+6.55% over AWQ at 3-bit): *assumes and profits from* "fine-tuning relocates quantization-critical weights" but **never measures a fragility map and never compares base-vs-fine-tuned sensitivity**. Simultaneously validates the premise and leaves the measurement unpublished.
- **Massive Activations** App A.2 (verified) — positions/values largely static under *chat*-tuning (one Mixtral exception); **no published check for reasoning distillation or domain SFT, and no super-weight-movement study exists** (searched directly; nothing found). Adjacent whole-model results: quantized-reasoning COLM 2025 repo has no localization; diffusion-LLM study (https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14896) finds instruct > base robustness, whole-model only.
- **The unclaimed measurement:** per-tensor k-quant KL fragility maps across a training lineage of one architecture — **Qwen2.5-1.5B base → Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B (SFT) → DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (reasoning distillation, built on the Math checkpoint), + Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B branch** — asking whether capability training relocates fragility, whether super weights move, and whether the base model's allocation transfers to its fine-tunes. Expect reviewers to cite 2510.06213 and QuantLRM as flanks; the claim is "first *localized* cross-stage maps," and either outcome (maps move / maps are training-invariant) is a first.

**Tensor-level damage taxonomy via capability micro-probes: open, flanked.**
- Nearest prior, all with the causal direction *reversed* (quantize whole model, observe where damage shows up): **Two Failure Modes** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19884 (signal degradation vs computation collapse; logit-lens + component diagnostics; factual recall only); **Through a Compressed Lens** (TrustNLP @ NAACL 2026) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13963 (knowledge-neuron attribution under quantization; late-layer concentration). Procedurally closest: **Ghosh, LessWrong Jul 2026** — https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/oJJyYDgPD95jEfvQx/linear-probes-tell-you-where-quantization-will-hurt — quantize-one-layer + linear probes (POS/NER/chunking), probe-guided mixed precision; encoder-centric, layer-level, blog post. Background (FFNs store facts): Geva 2021 (arXiv 2012.14913), ROME (2202.05262), knowledge neurons (2104.08696).
- **Unclaimed combination:** *causal* per-tensor k-quant damage × a multi-capability micro-probe battery (induction/copying for attention circuitry, factual cloze for FFN recall, arithmetic for composite computation) on decoder LLMs, crossed with training stage.

**Revised recommendation (supersedes §8's ranking):** headline = **"quantization fragility is conditional"** — on the task you probe (C2, tensor granularity) and on **training history** (C6/C7, the lineage design) — with capability micro-probes as the mechanism, equal-byte GGUF validation as proof it matters (own-map vs base-map vs stock on the R1-distill), pair typology as a refine-Hill secondary, and a measured-map-vs-ecosystem-proxies benchmark (imatrix statistics, weighted-MSE, SNR, noise-injection, ΔW — no published comparison exists, and eaddario's documented misfires prove it's needed) as the citable artifact. Startup wedge sharpened accordingly: **fine-tune-aware requantization** ("every public quant recipe was tuned for the base model; your fine-tune moved its fragile tensors") — a wedge with a customer in every org that fine-tunes, whose scientific leg is exactly what C6/C7 measure.

## 10. Addendum 2 (2026-08-28): external-review adjudication — two fact-checks and a reorder

**TAQ's 87.5% task-invariance figure is real — provenance now exact.** The reviewer could not find it; it is in **Appendix D.2** ("Mixed-task calibration and cross-task allocation stability") of arXiv 2511.06516: "87.5% of layer assignments remain the same across calibration tasks, with cross-task Spearman correlation ρ=0.757," with Figure 8's caption ("87.5% of layers receive identical bit-widths regardless of calibration task; task-specific variation concentrates at boundary blocks") and the authors' own conclusion that "many important layers are shared across tasks." Note also that TASA's Kendall τ≈0 compares **ppl-derived vs reasoning-derived** rankings (metric-conditioning), a different comparison from TAQ's task-vs-task ρ=0.757 — the two numbers do not contradict each other.

**Super-weight persistence under fine-tuning is measured — but only under vanilla LoRA, where it is near-tautological.** arXiv 2607.08733 ("Super Weights in LLMs and the Failure of Selective Training", OLMo-1B/7B; verified full text): Table 9 shows −0.03% mean super-weight magnitude change and full retention of top-percentile rank after **vanilla LoRA on attention matrices — with `down_proj`, the tensor that holds the super weights, left completely untrained**. Persistence under **full-parameter** SFT/distillation/continued-pretraining (our lineage's intervention types) remains unmeasured. Our tracking claim is downgraded to a cited footnote-scale observation accordingly — and this same result dictates that any *controlled* fine-tune added to the lineage must be full-parameter, or relocation will be null by construction.

**Claims the review made that our records already adjudicate:** "isolation-vs-marginal + additivity has never been done" is wrong at layer granularity — Hill (arXiv 2607.12266) measured joint-vs-sum exhaustively over layer lattices and built an allocator on it, and CoopQ (2509.15455) models pairwise interactions; the surviving lane is exactly the tensor-granular, typed/signed version this plan already scopes as refine-or-refute-Hill (§9). ## 11. Addendum 3 (2026-08-29): pipeline-novelty scoop-check

Three candidate pipeline upgrades were verified before entering the design (fetched 2026-08-29):

**Per-tensor damage propagation curves — OPEN, one crowded neighbor.** arXiv 2604.19884 runs a full depth-resolved diagnostic suite (logit lens, per-layer attention JSD, CKA, FP16-activation causal patching) — but only on *fully- or prefix-quantized* models; its per-component ablations (App. C.1) read out only endpoint accuracy. QEP (2504.09629) plots one aggregate prefix-quantized error-vs-depth curve as motivation, then compensates. 2506.12044 patches clean activations into whole-model-quantized models. Nobody produces a depth-resolved divergence curve *per single damaged tensor*, and nobody predicts pairwise interactions from propagation-path overlap — Hill (2607.12266) predicts interactions from a fitted set-function, CLADO (2307.05657, CNNs/ViTs) measures them by finite differences. Our claim: per-tensor curves (near-free given cached baseline activations) + path-overlap as an interaction *predictor*, benchmarked against Hill's coverage model.

**Susceptibility collapse (KL ≈ c_tensor × MSE_type) — SUBSTANTIALLY TAKEN; repositioned as a test.** The Linearity Theorem / HIGGS (arXiv 2411.17525, Nov 2024) proves and empirically validates per-layer linearity of perplexity damage in weight quantization MSE, fits measured per-layer coefficients by Gaussian noise injection + regression, identifies the ~3-bit breakdown boundary, and uses grid MSE to collapse quantizer choice for DP allocation. Countervailing evidence: PQI (2503.01901) measures second-order predictions underestimating true damage ~100× under *joint whole-model* quantization on Llama-3.2. What's open: does linearity hold under **real k-quant error distributions on unrotated weights** (not Gaussian-on-Hadamard), which **specific tensors** break it (a tensor-resolved failure map; HIGGS gives one global threshold), and reconciling the HIGGS-vs-PQI tension (isolated vs accumulated regime — exactly our Sweep A vs Sweep B axis). This costs zero extra data: Sweep A across four types + measured MSE of the spliced weights *is* the test. HIGGS's noise-injection coefficient also joins the C8 proxy benchmark as another live method to adjudicate.

**Prefix-cached scanning + adaptive measurement — open but low-weight.** No quantization-scan work starts forwards at the damage site from cached activations (KL Lens does full forwards; EvoPress full evaluations) — but the mechanic is routine in mechanistic-interp patching, so it's claimed as an efficiency import ("first applied to quantization scanning"), worth an exact ~2× on Sweep A. EvoPress already does staged multi-fidelity KL evaluation (fixed top-k schedule); the open delta is confidence-bound racing for *sensitivity ranking* — noted as future work, not this week.
